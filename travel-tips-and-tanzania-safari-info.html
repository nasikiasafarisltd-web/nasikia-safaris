Tanzania Tips and Informations – Overview

Best Time to Visit Tanzania

  • Dry Season (June to October): This is the best time for wildlife viewing, especially for the Great Migration in the Serengeti and game drives in the Ngorongoro Crater. It’s also ideal for climbing Mount Kilimanjaro.
  • Wet Season (November to May): The landscape is lush, making it a beautiful time for photography. The calving season in January and February is a unique opportunity to see newborn animals, especially wildebeest.

What to Pack for a Tanzania Safari

  • Clothing: Light, breathable layers are essential. Long sleeves and pants will protect you from the sun and insects. Neutral colors like khaki, green, and beige are recommended.
  • Footwear: Comfortable hiking boots for treks and sturdy sandals for camp.
  • Essentials: Sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat, binoculars, and a camera with extra batteries.
  • Travel Documents: Valid passport, visa (if required), and proof of Yellow Fever vaccination. Make copies of important documents.

Health and Safety Tips

  • Vaccinations and Medications: Ensure you have the necessary vaccinations, including Yellow Fever and Malaria prophylaxis. Carry a first-aid kit and any personal medications.
  • Wildlife Safety: Always listen to your guide’s instructions, maintain a safe distance from animals, and avoid sudden movements during game drives.
  • Water and Food: Drink only bottled or boiled water. Stick to well-cooked meals at lodges or camps, and avoid street food unless recommended by your guide.

Safari Etiquette

  • Respect Wildlife: Avoid making loud noises or sudden movements that may disturb animals.
  • Leave No Trace: Don’t litter in the parks, and respect the environment by sticking to designated trails.
  • Photography Etiquette: Be mindful of your fellow travelers when taking photos and avoid obstructing views. If you're capturing portraits of locals, always ask for permission first.

Cultural Considerations

  • Local Tribes: Tanzania is home to over 120 ethnic groups, including the Maasai, Chagga, and Hadzabe. If visiting villages, respect local customs, dress modestly, and support local economies by purchasing authentic handmade crafts.
  • Language: Swahili is the national language, but many people in the tourism industry speak English. Learning a few basic Swahili phrases like "Jambo" (Hello) and "Asante" (Thank you) can be a great way to connect with locals.

Visa and Entry Requirements

  • Visa: Most travelers will need a visa to enter Tanzania. You can obtain one in advance online or at your point of entry. Double-check requirements based on your nationality.
  • Passport Validity: Ensure your passport is valid for at least six months beyond your intended departure date and has sufficient blank pages for entry and exit stamps.

Currency and Tipping

  • Currency: The Tanzanian Shilling (TZS) is the local currency. US dollars are widely accepted in tourist areas, so it’s advisable to carry both.
  • Tipping Guidelines: Tipping is customary on safari. Expect to tip your guide around $10–$15 per person per day, and $5–$10 for camp staff. It’s a nice gesture to show appreciation for their hard work.

Getting Around in Tanzania

  • Internal Flights: Small charter flights are a common way to move between parks and cities. Be prepared for luggage weight restrictions.
  • Roads: Safari vehicles are often 4x4s, and drives between parks can be long. However, the scenery and wildlife make it worthwhile.
  • Transportation Tips: Arrange transfers with your tour operator for ease and safety. Public transportation is not recommended for tourists due to inconsistent schedules and safety concerns.

Photography Tips for Your Safari

  • Camera Gear: A DSLR or mirrorless camera with a telephoto lens (200mm or longer) is ideal for capturing wildlife. Bring extra memory cards and batteries.
  • Best Times for Photos: Early mornings and late afternoons provide the best light for photography and the most wildlife activity.
  • Respecting Wildlife: Use a zoom lens rather than approaching animals too closely for a good shot.

Internet and Connectivity

  • Wi-Fi: Most lodges and camps offer Wi-Fi in common areas, though the connection may be slow or intermittent.
  • Mobile Data: If you need more reliable connectivity, consider purchasing a local SIM card with data or an international data plan. Major providers include Vodacom, Airtel, and Tigo

Sustainability and Responsible Tourism

  • Support Local Communities: Choose lodges and safari operators that invest in local communities and wildlife conservation efforts.
  • Reduce Your Impact: Minimize single-use plastics, conserve water, and avoid purchasing products made from endangered wildlife.
